*NPS20011 Social Innovation Challenge, Swinburne University of Technology. Team PoleSTARS: Paul Tai, Eric Jose, Tassika Elliot and Matthew Freriks.*

I interviewed councils on their implementation, the pros of the data and the cons and where they thought the improvements could be made and where they think it was nailed. was also part of the poster creation building in adobe illustrator the poster we used to show our ideas and help build the final report for the implementation, where and how we would build these poles.

## The challenge

The City of Boroondara's Glenferrie Place Plan includes an "Innovation Spine" near Swinburne's Hawthorn campus. Our team proposed upgrading its street poles into smart poles that add value for the community and local businesses.

## The proposal

A single modular pole that combines several services:

- **Efficient lighting:** dimmable LED lights, with solar power where possible.
- **Connectivity:** public Wi-Fi, telecommunications and Internet of Things connections.
- **Safety:** cameras and features designed for crime prevention.
- **Charging points** for devices, scooters and electric vehicles.
- **Monitoring:** traffic flow and congestion, plus air quality, noise and weather.

We also planned how it would integrate with smart benches, smart bins and bike lanes, where poles should go, and how to involve industry and the community. We treated **privacy and data collection** as design considerations from the start, not afterthoughts, which matters for anything with cameras and sensors in public spaces.

## Working with stakeholders

We worked with the Boroondara Council team and interviewed a Brisbane City Council member about their experience with smart pole programs, which shaped what we included and how we presented the case.
