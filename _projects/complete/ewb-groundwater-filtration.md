## The problem

The EWB Challenge asks first-year engineering students to design solutions for a real community, set by Engineers Without Borders with a partner organisation. Our team focused on water supply. Drinking water had to be boiled to make it safe, which takes time and fuel, and the water could still carry iron, mud and bacteria.

## The concept

A groundwater filtration system with three parts:

- **A solar-powered pump** that draws groundwater up to the system, so it doesn't depend on mains power or fuel.
- **Raised storage tanks with filters** that remove iron, mud, bacteria and other contaminants.
- **Taps at the base** that deliver cheap, drinkable water straight to the people using it.

The goal was water that's **cleaner**, **needs no boiling** and is **faster** to collect than the old method.

![Concept drawing of the groundwater filtration system: a solar panel powers a pump, which feeds raised tanks with filters and taps below](/images/projects/ewb-groundwater-filtration-concept.jpg)

## From sketch to concept

I produced the concept drawings by hand, working from early sketches to a final illustrated page. The early drafts focused on simplifying the system into parts a community could build and maintain.

![An early hand-drawn draft of the concept, showing the solar panel, pump and tank frame in construction lines](/images/projects/ewb-early-sketch.jpg)

The research behind it covered drinking water contamination and treatment methods, solar-powered pumping, rainwater catchment and chlorination as alternatives, ion exchange, and the social factors that affect whether a community trusts and adopts a new water source.
